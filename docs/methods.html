01

Why email is the hard case

As far as I have been able to establish, nobody has ever published their own private mailbox.

Every email corpus available for training or evaluation exists because someone lost control of one. Enron came from a federal investigation, the Avocado collection from a company's liquidation, the FOIA sets from public-records law, the political archives from leaks. The consented exceptions are mailing lists, which are public by design and nothing like private correspondence: no terse replies, no context you had to be in the room for.

That is why a dataset from 2001 is still the field standard twenty-five years later, and it has a direct consequence for method. Retrieval techniques for email get validated on a corpus of internal energy-trading mail from a quarter of a century ago, or on somebody's private archive that no reviewer can see. This project does both, and tries to be exact about which claim came from which.

If you know of a corpus I have missed, please open an issue. I would like to be wrong about this.

02

Evaluation design

author-reported

The private ladder is scored on 360 questions against a ~32,000-email work archive, split three ways because the failure modes are different:

categorynwhat it tests
content144the answer sits in one message with enough words to match on
terse144the answering message is short and nearly contentless on its own
spanning72the answer genuinely needs several messages

Each question was generated from a known email, so the recall ladder scores against hard gold labels with no LLM judge anywhere in the loop. Generating a question from its own answer risks circularity, where the question simply echoes the target's rare tokens, so questions come from body content under a rule banning metadata questions, followed by a validation pass.

The load-bearing guard against that circularity is external rather than procedural. The same ordering of arms holds on public Enron-QA, whose questions were written by someone else entirely, and on TREC Legal's real human relevance judgments. A generator cannot flatter itself across three corpora it did not write.

Where a judge is used, and how far it is trusted

Answer quality is a separate lens and it does use a local LLM judge, calibrated against a stronger reference model on 514 pooled pairs: Cohen's κ = 0.52 on the 0–3 scale, Spearman 0.74. That is moderate agreement, not good agreement. The reason it is usable is narrower than the correlation: both pre-registered decisions came out identical under both judges. A judge that agrees on the decision you are about to make is worth more than a judge with a flattering κ.

03

Confounds that were caught

R8 author-reported

Two results changed materially once a control was run properly. Both are in the repo because they are the more useful half of the story.

A quantization artifact worth 3 points

An early headline put contextual summaries at +6pp. The control collection had been built with a summarizer at a different quantization from the treatment, so the comparison was measuring two things at once. Re-running the control at matched precision split it cleanly: +3pp from quantization, +3pp from the method. Holding the summarizer fixed is the difference between a result and a mirage.

A cross-dataset confound in the sparse number

Learned-sparse looked worth +4.8 R@5 on the private corpus and only +2.5 on public Enron, which is a suspicious gap for the same technique. The private collections carried embedded summaries and the public ones did not, so sparse was being credited with part of the summary's work. Building a body-only private control brought it to +3.3, in line with the public figure. The headline result survived the correction, which is the only reason it is still quoted.

04

Threading without headers

S3S4 reproducible

The whole system treats the conversation as the unit of truth, which assumes conversations can be recovered. On the standard public corpus they cannot be read off the headers at all.

Across 19,530 messages of the CMU Enron maildir, In-Reply-To and References appear on 0.0%. Not rarely. Zero. Yet 64.3% of messages are replies or forwards by subject, and deriving conversations from a normalised subject plus shared participants recovers 50.2% of messages into multi-message threads, the largest running to 59 messages.

That recall is bought at a price, and the price is measurable rather than hypothetical:

failure moderate
same-thread pairs sharing no participant (transitive chaining)2.2%
threads spanning more than 30 days11.1%
threads spanning more than a year1.4%
threaded messages under generic subjects2.3%

The worst case observed is a "happy hour" thread: 36 messages over 391 days across 16 participants, which is a recurring invitation rather than a conversation. Mail carrying real threading headers, meaning any live IMAP account, avoids the problem entirely. Reporting the derived-threading error rate matters because every thread-level number on public data inherits it.

What replicates in public

R1R2bR2c reproducible

Two of the ladder's levers can now be checked by a stranger on 1,200 public Enron emails with no API key. Both report a paired test, because both arms answer identical queries and the unpaired intervals overlap.

Contextual embedding, 99 single-message questions

indexR@1R@5R@10
plain37.4%60.6%74.7%
with thread context50.5%73.7%80.8%

Thread reconstruction, 73 spanning questions

The right conversation is found 97.3% of the time at top-5. The top-5 messages give you 52.6% of that conversation, and expanding to the thread gives you all of it. The gap between those two numbers is the argument for the whole design, and a single-message benchmark cannot see it.

The retrieval layer, 360 Enron-QA queries

Dense scores 94.4 R@5 [91.6, 96.4] against 97.5 [95.3, 98.7] for dense plus learned-sparse. Paired: 12 fixed, 1 broken, McNemar exact p = 0.0034. Widening the distractor pool fivefold makes the task harder and the sparse advantage larger, at +4.4pp (p = 0.0001). Direction under increasing difficulty is a more durable claim than a point estimate.

What none of this reproduces is magnitude on a real mailbox. A 32,000-email archive is a harder target than 1,200 messages, and the 45.6 → 93.3 ladder stays author-reported.

06

The winner flips with the task

R7 author-reported low power

Running the same two stacks on legal e-discovery reverses the ranking. On three TREC Legal topics with real human relevance judgments, a general-purpose dense-plus-rerank stack wins, and the learned-sparse hybrid that leads on email trails it.

armP@10
general-purpose dense + rerank0.500
general-purpose dense0.367
bge-m3 hybrid + rerank0.300
bge-m3 hybrid0.200

The obvious objection is the correct one, so it goes first: that stack is built and tuned for general-purpose retrieval, and it is very good at it. E-discovery asks broad topical questions with many relevant documents, which is what reranking is for. Email asks pinpoint questions with specific terms, which is what learned-sparse is for. The finding is that specialising for a task beats general-purpose on that task, and email is a task worth specialising for.

Three topics is low statistical power, one topic bucked the trend, and the absolute P@10 values are low because e-discovery is hard. Read it as a direction. The register also marks this row unverifiable until its collections are rebuilt, so it is the weakest evidence on this page and is labelled accordingly.

07

Negative results, and one withdrawal

R6b withdrawn

Three techniques that the literature and the tutorials both recommend did not earn their place here, and one of them produced the only published claim this project has had to withdraw.

The withdrawal

Reranking helps pointed questions but demotes the answer on thread-spanning ones.

Re-running that arm over 360 queries found the reranker neutral or positive on recall in every category. Thread-spanning is unchanged at rank 5 and better at rank 1.

categorynhybrid → +rerank, E@5E@1
content14460.4 → 67.4 (+7.0)+1.4
terse14458.3 → 57.6 (−0.7, one query)+8.3
spanning7270.8 → 70.8 (0.0)+6.9

The demotion was real, but it belonged to a different measurement: the LLM-judged answer-quality eval, where reranking hurt every category. It had been restated next to a recall figure until the two read as one result. Different metric, different run, and the error was mine. Reranking is still off by default on the answer-quality finding.

The row stays in the register rather than being deleted, and it stays on this page rather than in a footnote. A reader who saw the old wording should be able to find out what replaced it.

Two that simply did not pay

Cross-encoder reranking adds +2.5 R@5 overall for a per-query call to a paid endpoint, which is a poor trade next to thread reconstruction's +29.1 for no LLM at all. Query-side HyDE never beat the raw query on this corpus: the query already holds the entity anchors that matter, so a hypothetical document can only echo them or add drift. Both remain in the tree, off by default, for corpora shaped differently from this one.

When the metadata lies: four signals that failed

A1–A3 author-reported

Email attachments are mostly not attachments. On a real 45,454-row mailbox, 61% of them are decoration — signature blocks, legal disclaimers rendered as images, newsletter headers, spacer pixels. Filtering them out sounds like a five-minute job. Every cheap way of doing it was wrong, and each one was caught only by opening the images rather than trusting what the database said about them.

signalwhy it fails
filename / typeimage002.png is a 259-byte spacer in one message and a 12 MB pasted screenshot in another. Both image/png, both inline.
aspect ratio"Banner-shaped means signature" — the largest banner turned out to be a product-lifecycle table with end-of-life dates.
reuse across messagesAn image quoted down an 18-message reply chain counts 18 times. This wrongly removed 36% of what it touched, including a feature-request table.
reuse across threadsBetter — but a benchmark table shared into five threads because it is useful still looks like a logo.

What works is measuring the text the image actually contains: a logo reads as a company name or nothing, a table reads as dozens of words and numbers. Cheap, too — 0.09s per image, and keyed by content hash, so the one logo carried by 2,273 messages is measured once.

Calibrating that produced two corrections in opposite directions. A pasted "401 Access is denied" screenshot measures 29 characters and is content, so text-poverty alone cannot condemn. And a confidentiality disclaimer rendered as an image measures 748 characters across 829 threads — text-rich, and certainly not content. Nothing genuine appears in that many unrelated conversations.

One more trap, found only by benchmarking the two OCR engines against each other: the vision model prefixes a description before its transcription, so the same three-word header measures 22 characters via tesseract and 159 via the LLM — across the threshold that decides the verdict. A rule that is a threshold over an extractor's output has the extractor as part of the rule.

A band between 15 and 25 threads stays genuinely ambiguous — a real reporting-deadline table sits at 15, a disclaimer at 18 — so the cut errs toward keeping. Some signature images survive rather than one real document being hidden.

08

The register

reproducibleauthor-reported

Most published numbers come from one-off scripts run months ago against a corpus nobody else can see. Without a register, "is that still true?" has no answer, and the honest answer drifts as the code, the models and the dependencies move underneath it.

So every figure this project publishes has a row recording what produced it, which corpus it came from, whether a stranger can reproduce it, and the date the producing script was last run rather than last quoted. Rows older than the most recent release should not be quoted without a caveat, and two rows currently carry a warning: one needs an endpoint, one needs its collections rebuilt.

The register also caught something a checklist would have missed. When the public benchmark shipped, its docs listed two omissions and missed a third: contextual summaries were switched off too, so a reader could reasonably have assumed the +12.8 lever was in play. Writing every claim down next to the thing that produces it is how that class of gap gets found.
